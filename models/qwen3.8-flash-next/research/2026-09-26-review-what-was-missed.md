## Summary

The review found 45 distinct gaps in what the tuning tried, measured or can support: **17 keep**, **25 keep-as-question**, **3 drop**. The largest untested lever for a normal turn is not a kernel: nobody measured how much of each turn's decode is reasoning. Several published numbers do not survive their own raw files, and the acceptance and KL (Kullback-Leibler divergence) gates used to judge changes have unknown power.

Platform items sit in the section of what they affect: `restart-warmup-penalty` and `power-cap-throttle` under prefill, `l0-submission-knobs` under decode, `platform-hygiene` under operations. The two dropped platform items are under [Dropped](#dropped).

Headline findings:

- **Reasoning share is unmeasured.** A turn at 135K spends about 7 s decoding against about 0.8 s of prompt (session log, not archived), yet nothing splits reasoning from answer tokens (`reasoning-share-and-budget`).
- **A step-time jump sits inside the agent's range.** Production-journal fits showed ms per MTP step jumping by 20-28 ms between about 106K and 112K ([Qwen sparse attention (QSA) design note](2026-09-25-long-context-qsa-design.md)). No build since has been measured across it (`step-jump-106-112k`).
- **Decode levers are chosen blind.** Today's step is 68.0-70.3 ms ([windows CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-cold-read-windows.csv), [draft QSA experiment](../experiments/2026-09-25-mtp-draft-qsa.md)); the launch count behind every decode estimate is a hand count ([decode plan](2026-09-24-decode-bottleneck-and-speed-plan.md)) (`decode-step-decomposition`).
- **Some published claims fail against the raw files:** the `eh_proj` short-turn and acceptance lines, the 15% of the score-expansion broadcast step, "the draft head never changes the output", and every "within noise" KL verdict for a draft or verify change ([Correctness and methodology](#correctness-and-methodology)). This repository's experiment files already qualify the first two.
- **Recovery depends on a clean stop.** A crash cannot save the prompt cache ([restart finding](../../../findings/llama-server-restart-drops-prefix-cache.md)), and during one recovery the client-facing endpoint silently served a different model for about 10 minutes (session log, not archived) (`outage-root-causes`).

### How to read this note

- **Verdicts.** keep = real gap, worth doing as stated. keep-as-question = plausible gap whose value only a measurement can decide. drop = not worth doing; the reason is under [Dropped](#dropped).
- **Estimates.** Every expected effect is an estimate unless it says "measured". "Derived" means arithmetic on the cited numbers.
- **Sources.** Raw CSVs, experiment, benchmark and research files, patches and tools of this repository are linked. References to the [tuning inventory](./2026-09-26-tuning-inventory.md) name the inventory item in quotes. "(session log, not archived)" marks a number whose only source is a test-window output, a service-journal excerpt, the host's own README or the session transcript; none of those is in this repository.
- **Upstream.** PR states and web figures were fetched by the reviewers on 2026-09-26 and are not re-verified here. Third-party numbers are UNVERIFIED on our hardware.
- **Builds.** Hashes are commits of our llama.cpp patch series ([patches README](../configs/patches/README.md)): `2c88bdf19` score expansion by broadcast (0011), `517eb833d` and `85b26781b` grouped MoE (mixture of experts) GEMM (general matrix multiply) on XMX (Xe Matrix Extensions) with vector loads and in-place I/O (0014, 0015), `a890bf8b0` `eh_proj` as one 2D product (0016), `2b84213a4` production (0017).

## Speed levers not tried: prefill and prompt-turn latency

Ordered by value for the workload.

### Attribute the 0.76 s short turn and the time outside `prompt_ms`; time the chat path (`ttft-non-model-split`)

**keep** · speed: prefill · effort: hours · top-12 rank 7

- **What:** Split a short turn's TTFT (time to first token) into server compute, host work and the HTTP path, and time the `/v1/chat/completions` path the client actually uses.
- **Why:** A turn of about 60 new tokens at 135K takes 0.76 s on the production code ([windows CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-cold-read-windows.csv), row `bcp5hgsyz`). On the raw `/completion` path 0.15-0.24 s per request sits outside `prompt_ms` (measured). Two checks: `lc-poolA` turn 1 spent 1.22 s in prompt and decoded 256 tokens at 47.1 tok/s, 6.66 s of server time ([per-turn CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-lcbench-sessions-per-turn.csv)), against 6.8 s of wall time; a prompt-only profiling run sent 40 requests in 52 s against a 1,074 ms `prompt_ms` median (both wall figures: session log, not archived). No long-context turn has gone through `/v1/chat/completions`, the Jinja chat template and the two proxy hops. The inventory records the steps that took this turn from 1.24-1.27 s to 0.76 s ([inventory](./2026-09-26-tuning-inventory.md), "Why ~60-token prompt turns took over 1 s at 135K"), but nothing splits the remaining 0.76 s.
- **Caveats:** [`lcbench.py`](../../../tools/lcbench.py) prints wall time at 0.1 s, so each gap is good to about ±0.05 s. The host-timing `set_inputs` figures (target 31.9-36.7 ms, draft 17.9 ms) average prompt micro-batches of more than 8 tokens and do not describe a 60-token turn; the about 294 ms per-turn op bound predates `eh_proj` (both: session log, not archived).
- **Expected effect (estimate):** 15-30% of a 0.76 s turn is outside the server's prompt time (measured on the raw path). Host trims could cut 0.2-0.4 s per short turn.
- **How to test:** In a test window with the saved slot held (`hold` in the [slot-cache script](../configs/llama-server-slot-cache.sh)): record client wall time per turn in the lcbench JSON and print wall − (`prompt_ms` + `predicted_ms`). Send 6 turns each through `/completion` and through `/v1/chat/completions` (a messages array of about 135K tokens, `--jinja`, the production sampler, both proxy hops); time `/tokenize` separately. Then one prompt-only profile on `2b84213a4` with `LLAMA_HOST_PROF=20` and `GGML_SYCL_OP_PROFILE=20`, reading the t≤8 and t=1 rows, divided by the request count.

### Prefill: at least 36% of compute wall time is outside SYCL op scopes; re-profile the host side (`prefill-host-gap`)

**keep-as-question** · speed: prefill · effort: hours to measure; the fix depends on the finding · top-12 rank 11

- **What:** Profile the host during one cold 135K read on the production build, and test whether lazily paged PLE (per-layer embedding) rows cost time early in a read.
- **Why:** On `85b26781b` the op profiler counted 13,246 ms inside op scopes over 37 graph computes, while host timing counted 18,663.4 ms of target plus 1,995.7 ms of draft compute: 64% inside op scopes, 61% and 66% in the next windows (session log, not archived). The op profiler serializes ops, so the 36% outside is a lower bound. On `a890bf8b0` the segment from 92,160 to 122,880 tokens took 78.85 s against 46.47 s for the first 30,720 ([progress CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-cold-read-progress.csv), run 01:36:43), while the MoE matmuls cost a fixed ~0.50 s per 1536-token micro-batch (144 calls at 3.42-3.57 ms, [prefill op profile CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-prefill-op-profile.csv); derived). The PLE table is read lazily from the memory-mapped GGUF model file ([launch script](../configs/llama-server-262k-mtp.sh) comment), 111 GB on a host with 128 GB of RAM and a Gen3 NVMe at about 3.4 GB/s ([host](../../../hardware/hosts/quad-b70-5800x-pex88096.md)). The only host profile of prefill predates the grouped GEMM ([prefill note](2026-09-25-prefill-bottleneck.md)); the planned re-profile never ran ([inventory](./2026-09-26-tuning-inventory.md), "Speed-plan prerequisites P3, P4, P5").
- **Caveats:** The two profilers' windows are offset by about 4 s, and the host averages mix 1536- and 512-token micro-batches (session log, not archived). Second reads in one server life were faster, but with different prompts (see `restart-warmup-penalty`).
- **Expected effect (estimate):** at most about 100 s of a 284 s cold read (36% of 284 s, a lower-bound share; derived). How much is removable is unknown until measured. Helps cold reads after compaction, deploys and restore misses, not normal turns.
- **How to test:** In a held test window during one cold read: `perf record --call-graph dwarf` on the main thread at about 60 s and 200 s, `perf stat` major and minor faults, per-card GPU busy, per-micro-batch wall time against host-timed compute at low and high context. For PLE: two salted cold reads in one server life, comparing the 0-30,720-token segment, with `vmtouch` or `fincore` on the PLE shard before and after. Also A/B `-ub 512` against `-ub 1536` at `-b 2048`.

### First-request-after-restart penalty in headline cold reads; confirm, then warm up (`restart-warmup-penalty`)

**keep-as-question** · platform · effort: hours

- **What:** Confirm whether the first 135K read after a server start is slower than a second one, and add a warm-up request if it is.
- **Why:** The second 135K read in the same server life reached 122,880 tokens sooner on every tree ([progress CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-cold-read-progress.csv); trees from the [windows CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-cold-read-windows.csv)):

  | Tree | First read at 122,880 tokens | Second read | Gap |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | dev tree before `2c88bdf19` | 337.12 s | 317.26 s | 19.9 s |
  | `517eb833d` | 274.07 s | 242.56 s | 31.5 s |
  | `85b26781b` | 275.10 s | 233.31 s | 41.8 s |
  | `a890bf8b0` | 247.51 s | 238.12 s | 9.4 s |

  Every second read is the KL probe's unsalted prompt, so each pair compares different prompts. On `517eb833d` the gap grows through the read (19.4 s at 30,720 tokens, 31.5 s at 122,880), which does not fit a one-off JIT (just-in-time compilation) cost. The persistent SYCL kernel cache stays off on the strength of one community report of about 30 s of JIT warm-up per start ([finding](../../../findings/no-persistent-sycl-cache-on-bmg.md)). The service accepts traffic right after start (session log, not archived); llama-server's own small warm-up runs, since the [launch script](../configs/llama-server-262k-mtp.sh) does not pass `--no-warmup`.
- **Expected effect (estimate):** 9.4 s on the production code, 3.3-3.8% of the headline read (derived); the larger gaps belong to older trees. The user-facing effect is about 3 s, because a slot restore avoids most cold reads after a restart (first turn 3.92 s against about 0.8 s, [slot-cache CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-slot-cache-timings.csv)). Main value: build comparisons stop including start-up cost.
- **How to test:** (a) Two salted 135K cold reads of the same prompt back to back in one server life, with the per-card clock sampler, to confirm the penalty and rule out thermals. (b) Only if (a) shows one: a warm-up request before the service takes traffic (a prompt over 32 rows, one full 1536-token micro-batch, 16 MTP-decoded tokens); afterwards read A should match read B within about 0.5%. (c) Optional: `SYCL_CACHE_PERSISTENT=1` over three restarts, gated by the KL probe within the same-build floor and an identical short sanity answer.

### A/B the three dense prompt flash-attention library paths (`prompt-fa-path-ab`)

**keep-as-question** · speed: prefill · effort: hours; no code change

- **What:** Force each of the three prompt FA (flash attention) paths with environment variables and compare.
- **Why:** Dense prompt FA is the largest prompt op on `85b26781b`: 9,885.4 ms over 255 calls, 38.77 ms per call, 32.8% of listed prompt op time at about 90-110K ([prefill op profile CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-prefill-op-profile.csv)). Over a whole 135K read attention is about 12% (session estimate, UNVERIFIED; [tile-union note](2026-09-26-sparse-prompt-attention-tile-union.md)). Prompt FA with 32 or more query rows takes oneDNN SDPA (scaled dot-product attention) first, which waits on the host after every call when more than one device is present (source reading of `fattn-onednn.cpp`; session log, not archived). No A/B of the paths exists.
- **Caveats:** The closest upstream analogue, llama.cpp [#29171](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29171) (a different head shape), reports +4.6% at pp8192 (1583.9 to 1657.1 tok/s) as fetched on 2026-09-26, not the 3x a candidate cited (UNVERIFIED here). That prompt FA is compute-bound on masked cells is unverified.
- **Expected effect (estimate):** direction unknown. A 30% cut in FA time is about 4% of a cold read (from the 12% share). Zero engineering cost.
- **How to test:** Three runs of the ~60 s prefill profile window: default; `GGML_SYCL_FA_ONEDNN_MIN_Q` raised above 1536 (forces the oneMKL (Math Kernel Library) FA block); the same plus `GGML_SYCL_ENABLE_MKL_FA=0` (reaches TILE). The variable names come from source reading (UNVERIFIED). Compare `FLASH_ATTN_EXT` ms per call, progress-line segment times, and the KL probe against the `kl-eh` run.

### int8 XMX path for the grouped MoE GEMM, which is bound by A-stage dequantization; aligned Q8_0 layout (`int8-mmq-grouped-gemm`)

**keep** · speed: prefill · effort: days (3-6) for the int8 path with tests; about 1 day for the Q8_0 layout

- **What:** (a) An MMQ-style (quantized matrix multiply) variant of the grouped GEMM: quantize the packed activations to int8 once per call with per-32-block scales and sums, unpack the K-quant, Q5_1 and Q8_0 weights to int8 without per-value float math, multiply s8 × s8 into s32 on XMX, and finish with an f32 epilogue. (b) For the five Q8_0 `down_exps` layers ([cost breakdown](2026-09-26-grouped-moe-gemm-cost-breakdown.md)), a row view over the per-expert `[qs][d]` layout of llama.cpp [#29337](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29337), so the Q8_0 A stage can use 16-byte loads (`block_q8_0` is 34 bytes, 2-byte aligned).
- **Why:** The kernel is bound by dequantizing weights into the A tiles, not by XMX. On `85b26781b`, skipping the A stage takes the Q4_K gate from 4,045.93 to 2,733.94 µs (A stage 1,311.99 µs, 32.4%) and the Q8_0 down from 7,436.62 to 2,761.55 µs (4,675.07 µs, 62.9%); skipping the XMX multiply-add saves only 368.23 µs (9.1%) on Q4_K. With the A stage skipped, all four types converge to 2,622-2,762 µs ([kernel diag CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-mul-mat-id-kernel-diag.csv)). The achieved rate is 12.81 TFLOPS for Q4_K and 6.81 for Q8_0, and the vector-load patch left Q8_0 unchanged, 7,970.92 to 7,981.60 µs ([kernel perf CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-mul-mat-id-kernel-perf.csv), [patch 0014](../configs/patches/0014-sycl-grouped-moe-gemm-vector-loads.diff)). Nothing like an int8 path is in the record.
- **Expected effect (estimate):** (a) kernel −20-30%, about −7-10% of prompt kernel time, since MoE is 35.5% of listed prompt op time at about 100K ([prefill op profile CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-prefill-op-profile.csv)) and 26-29% in other windows of the same build (session log, not archived). The wall-time effect is diluted by host time; this is a cold-read lever, not a per-turn one. (b) The Q8_0 layout alone saves about 18 ms per 1536-token micro-batch, under 1% of the target compute per micro-batch.
- **How to test:** `test-backend-ops perf` on the four production shapes with the diagnostic bits; correctness 60/60 with the reorder on and off; KL probe against `kl-eh` (int8 activations change numerics); cold-read progression. UNVERIFIED: int8 `joint_matrix` support on the B70, and the numerics of int8 activations.

### A/B q8_0 K/V for prefill attention bandwidth (`q8-kv-cache`)

**keep-as-question** · speed: prefill · effort: hours (one drop-in, one cold start, lcbench plus KL probe)

- **What:** One lcbench session (cold 135K read, 6 turns) and a KL probe with `-ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0` appended to the launch flags, against the f16 baseline of the same build.
- **Why:** Deferred without a measured reason. Production keeps an f16 KV (key/value) cache and "quantized KV was not tested here" ([configs README](../configs/README.md)); the only argument against it was a VRAM estimate ([inventory](./2026-09-26-tuning-inventory.md), "q8_0 KV cache"). On a B70, a community run found q8_0 faster at depth ([#26689](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/26689), UNVERIFIED here; [quants note](2026-09-24-quants-and-uncensored-variants.md)). On `85b26781b` each prompt FA node takes 247.6-270.3 ms over 7-8 calls per micro-batch (session log, not archived).
- **Caveats:** A 1536-row query micro-batch reuses each K/V tile many times, so prompt FA may be compute-bound and gain nothing. Whether the indexer key cache follows `-ctk` is unverified. The public [launch script](../configs/llama-server-262k-mtp.sh) writes `kv=f16/f16` into the slot signature regardless of `EXTRA_ARGS`, so hold or remove the saved slot before the q8_0 arm; a restore into a q8_0 cache is untested. Can share the `step-jump-106-112k` window.

### Device-side MoE expert routing and tile schedule; the oneDNN FA host wait (`device-side-routing`)

### Close the 2026-09-25 outage causes (`outage-root-causes`)

### Cost of a side request under `-np 1`; serialize the pooled rows; record the `-np 2` rejection (`side-request-swap-cost`)

**keep-as-question** · operations · effort: hours to measure; 1-2 days to serialize the pooled rows; minutes for client routing if the client supports it · top-12 rank 6

- **What:** Measure what a side request from the client (a title, a compaction, a sub-agent) costs the main conversation with one slot; serialize the pooled indexer rows in the slot state; write down why `-np 2` was rejected.
- **Why:** The first turn after a restore takes 3.92 s, then 0.78-0.80 s ([slot-cache CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-slot-cache-timings.csv)): a full state read invalidates the pooled QSA key cache, which rebuilds on that turn ([restart finding](../../../findings/llama-server-restart-drops-prefix-cache.md), [patch 0007](../configs/patches/0007-qwen4exp-pooled-qsa-key-cache.diff)). A displaced conversation coming back from the host-RAM cache takes the same path (estimate). `--cache-ram 24576` has one timed reload, about 40K tokens in 0.58 s (session log, not archived; [inventory](./2026-09-26-tuning-inventory.md), "Host-RAM prompt cache"); the 135K case is unmeasured ([restart finding](../../../findings/llama-server-restart-drops-prefix-cache.md)). The pooled fast path needs one stream, one sequence and unique positions ([patch 0009](../configs/patches/0009-qwen4exp-mtp-draft-qsa.diff) comment). With the pooled path off, 135K decode falls from 48.5 to 29.9 tok/s (−38%) and the step rises from 72.4 to 95.1 ms (+31%) ([summary CSV](../benchmarks/raw/2026-09-26-lcbench-sessions-summary.csv), `lc-poolA` against `lc-pooloff`): that is the cost of `-np 2` short of halving the context. VRAM at 135K (28,490-29,278 MiB per card of 31.89 GiB; session log, not archived) leaves no room for a second full-size slot.
- **Caveats:** How often side requests displace the agent is unknown; the one journal hint, a 247-token prompt save, is weak evidence (session log, not archived). Fix (b) pays off regardless, because it removes the 3.92 s after every restart.
- **Expected effect (estimate):** the first turn after each restore, and after each displacement, from 3.92 s to about 0.8 s (from the measured endpoints).
- **How to test:** With the 135K slot held: agent turn, one unrelated ~300-token and one ~5K-token `/completion`, agent turn; record that turn's `prompt_ms` and wall time, the prompt-cache save and load lines and `MemAvailable`; count save and load events in a day of real traffic (`journal-instrumentation`). Re-time the post-restore turn (3.92 s today, about 0.8 s expected after the fix); the KL probe must not change.
